About the toolkit

Decision support for an FPL Draft season

This site turns public FPL and FPL Draft data for this league into season-long decision support: free agents, fixtures, injuries, stash candidates, roster value, add/drop comparisons, a recommended starting XI and opponent-aware H2H matchup intelligence.

How it works

A scheduled GitHub Actions job collects Draft ownership, public FPL player status and Premier League fixtures. It keeps small ownership and health snapshots, calculates player intelligence, compares free agents with your roster, builds a legal Recommended XI, resolves the upcoming H2H opponent from the Draft league schedule and publishes the processed results here.

Scoring Gameweek vs Decision Gameweek: once a Gameweek deadline passes, its submitted lineups and transactions are locked. The toolkit keeps that round visible for live scoring, final results and model evaluation, but immediately moves Recommended XI, waivers, injuries and stashes, H2H scouting and the four-Gameweek planner to the next actionable round. For example, while GW1 is live, advice begins with GW2 and the planning window covers GW2–GW5.

Current decision model

v0.6.0 preserves a durable 2025/26 preseason performance and role baseline before the live FPL statistics reset for the new season. Once 2026/27 begins, completed-match evidence is blended gradually with that prior instead of replacing it after a single match. Partial minutes from a match in progress are excluded from the role estimate, preventing live-match churn across Recommended XI, waivers and H2H.

Recent Match Evidence v1 adds position-relative grades from the four newest official Gameweeks marked both finished and data-checked. Points carry the most weight, with BPS, expected involvement and minutes providing supporting evidence. Newer Gameweeks count more, thin samples receive low confidence, and the resulting adjustment is capped at ±5 model points. A double Gameweek is currently one aggregate grade. If the feed is unavailable, the adjustment is neutral and collection continues.

Short historical samples are pulled toward a conservative 70% start and 60-minute fallback, so a handful of starts cannot be treated as a guaranteed 90-minute role. The model separates Floor—reliable usage and production—from Upside—rate production, attacking involvement and future fixtures. Sample confidence describes how much playing-time evidence supports a comparison; it is not a probability that the recommendation is correct.

What the primary waiver labels mean

  • SWAP NOW: a clear same-position improvement with adequate evidence and no meaningful loss of immediate floor.
  • STASH SWAP: reserved for a genuine stash situation: the incoming player has a short-term availability cost, but enough future value and upside to justify acting early.
  • CONSIDER: a positive upgrade exists, but the separation or confidence is not strong enough for a firm recommendation.
  • KEEP ROSTER: the best comparison does not justify changing the roster.

Standalone strength

The secondary player-level label is deliberately non-transactional. Strong means the player grades well in isolation, Future value highlights a stash-type profile, Watch means interesting but not yet compelling, and Depth means the current standalone value is limited. The waiver label above it is the actual roster decision.

Floor vs Upside: Floor favors players likely to keep producing usable minutes. Upside rewards players whose rate production and attacking involvement indicate more potential than their raw season total may show.

Role evidence: HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW reflects how much historical playing-time evidence exists. Expected minutes remains a heuristic until enough 2026/27 evidence exists to calibrate a true start model.

Health & Transfers · v1.1

This dashboard is intentionally a decision view, not a directory of every unavailable or transfer-linked Premier League player. Your squad health identifies whether an injured player should be held or reviewed. Stash radar shows only free agents who clear a transaction or monitor guardrail. Return window groups worthwhile dated returns inside the current four-Gameweek horizon. Transfer window adds expiring, source-linked transfer evidence when it changes a lineup, waiver or early-pickup decision.

Health availability and player news come from the public official FPL feed. When that news contains a readable expected-return date, the toolkit maps it to the first fixture Gameweek on or after that date and calculates Post-return Fixtures using only fixtures from that Gameweek onward. Fixtures that occur while the player is expected to remain unavailable are not credited toward the Stash Score. A dated return beyond the current horizon receives no fixture-window credit.

Transfer guardrails: an official or reliably reported agreed exit from the Premier League suppresses selection and acquisition advice while FPL catches up. A confirmed same-league move can use the destination fixture matrix immediately. EARLY PICKUP requires a destination Fixture Score of at least 65, an improvement of at least 5 points over the current run and a projected-starter or strong-rotation role. MOVE WATCH does not recommend a transaction. Talks and rumours are displayed but never change a score. Every record expires and links to its evidence.

STASH is a strong standalone injured-player profile; STASH SWAP means the same-position add/drop comparison also clears the roster guardrails; MONITOR means the return profile is relevant but does not justify a move yet. HOLD protects one of your existing players when the availability cost is outweighed by medium-term value. Unknown return dates are treated conservatively, and the toolkit never invents a medical timeline.

Recommended XI · v0.6.3

The Recommended XI is a separate toolkit recommendation and is never presented as your submitted Draft lineup. It searches all legal FPL formations—one goalkeeper, 3–5 defenders, 2–5 midfielders and 1–3 forwards—and selects the highest-scoring legal XI for the next actionable Gameweek.

Start Score is a transparent selection heuristic, not projected FPL points. It combines current availability, the calibrated expected-minutes estimate, the immediate Gameweek fixture difficulty, Floor and Upside. Availability is applied conservatively so a ruled-out player cannot win a starting place purely from historical quality.

Evidence adjustment: a LOW-evidence player can still start, but a short historical sample must show a clearer advantage before displacing a better-established option. v0.6.3 also stabilizes role estimates while a match is live and blends completed early-season minutes gradually with the prior.

Close calls: when a starter and a same-position bench alternative are within 2.0 Start Score, the site flags the choice as marginal rather than presenting the ranking as decisive. The reserve goalkeeper remains separate and the three outfield substitutes are ordered by Start Score for autosub priority.

Pitch presentation: official FPL team codes request the corresponding FPL shirt artwork for the Recommended XI and bench. If an external shirt asset cannot be loaded, the site falls back automatically to a lightweight local shirt placeholder. Pitch cards intentionally show only player name, next fixture and Start Score; role evidence remains available in the player detail view and hover text.

H2H Scout + Projected Points · v1.3

The H2H view acts as an opponent scouting report. It resolves the opponent roster, builds a legal likely XI for both sides and estimates each XI's next-Gameweek points. Projected Points v1.0 starts from the toolkit's blended historical/live points-per-90, scales it by expected minutes, applies current availability and a bounded official-FPL fixture-difficulty adjustment, then widens the displayed uncertainty band when role evidence is weaker.

Transfer eligibility: ownership and projection are separate facts. An opponent may still officially own a player whom reliable transfer evidence has blocked from selection. That player remains in the ownership record for accuracy but receives zero Start Score, expected minutes, projected points, uncertainty range and effective H2H roster contribution. They cannot appear in the likely XI when a legal active alternative exists, current threat cards or four-Gameweek key threats. Every downstream H2H consumer enforces this guardrail independently so stale pre-transfer scores cannot leak back into the assessment.

Four-Gameweek H2H Outlook v1.1 resolves the exact league opponent for every Gameweek in the current planning window and projects a legal XI for both sides. It shows the projected score, uncertainty bands, matchup edge, weakest position and main opponent threat for each round. The summary identifies the toughest matchup, best opportunity and any positional weakness repeated across the schedule.

Progressive scouting v1.2: the live result, four-Gameweek outlook and current decision remain visible at a glance. Opponent profiling, full projected lineups, threats and tactical priorities sit in three expandable sections so the evidence remains available without overwhelming the initial view. On smaller screens, summary tiles and Gameweek cards scroll horizontally to reduce page length.

Opponent decisions v1.3: the toolkit retains aggregate add/drop quality and, after each completed round, compares submitted lineups with the best legal XI that was available from that squad. The supplied 90-pick draft history provides a small early-season prior. Draft influence is capped at ±0.6 future points; live transfer and lineup evidence can gradually raise the total adjustment, which remains capped at ±2.0 points. Activity alone is not rewarded, small samples are pulled toward neutral, and the active Gameweek forecast is never rewritten.

Future cards use every manager's current roster plus the explicitly labelled decision-profile adjustment. They cannot know an opponent's unsubmitted waiver requests, trades or future lineup choices. Cards refresh after every collection, while the active Gameweek retains the forecast captured when outcome tracking began. The projection is deliberately conservative and transparent. The uncertainty band is not a statistical confidence interval and the toolkit does not display a win probability.

Outcome diagnostics v0.1 freezes the first forecast captured for each Gameweek, follows the live H2H and Official Lineup scores, and reconciles them when the round finishes. The Draft league endpoint can leave a live matchup at 0–0 until it settles; while that happens the dashboard explicitly labels an estimate using your official XI against the opponent's likely XI. It switches to the authoritative league score when that becomes available. Only a forecast captured before any match starts is eligible for formal calibration. A first-time mid-Gameweek baseline—such as the initial GW1 rollout—is shown for transparency but clearly excluded from the calibration history.

Opponent scouting highlights likely formation, strongest and weakest position groups, bench depth, role uncertainty, availability concerns, weakest likely starter and the three main threats. Position cards compare projected points as well as Start Score.

Change urgency is LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH / VERY HIGH. It is based primarily on the projected XI gap, but transaction advice remains constrained by the normal waiver engine and season-long Roster Value. A short-term H2H deficit is never sufficient on its own to justify a damaging roster move. When a free agent is supported by the waiver model, the H2H Scout simulates the resulting legal XI and shows both the projected-GW gain and Roster Value change.

Privacy: the public H2H view shows each manager's chosen team name because it is their visible league identity. Real manager names and internal Draft entry identifiers remain removed from the public Pages dataset. If no team name is available, the dashboard falls back to “League opponent”.

Decision Updates · v1.0

The collector compares every four-hour run with the previous decision-state snapshot but now retains material findings for the entire actionable Gameweek. Recommended XI role changes, meaningful availability or expected-minutes movement, waiver-action changes, newly released free agents, Planner shifts and H2H signal changes therefore remain visible after later unchanged collections. Small score noise and transient live-match model movement are deliberately ignored.

Decision-cycle lifecycle: active updates are separated into Action needed and Worth monitoring. A later signal in the same category resolves the earlier card instead of erasing it, and claimed free-agent opportunities move into Resolved and recent. When the decision Gameweek advances, the completed cycle moves into a compact archive; the two most recent cycles are retained.

Grouped player updates: separate machine-readable events for the same player are grouped into one decision card and assigned the highest priority of their underlying events. Each card records when it first appeared. The navigation badge is stored locally per browser and counts unseen actionable updates rather than every retained card.

Overview · v1.0

Overview is the default homepage and owns the large Gameweek summary. It combines only decision signals already produced by the specialist models: active critical or important updates relevant to your squad or supported waiver candidates, act-now availability decisions, guarded early-pickup transfer alerts, HIGH or VERY HIGH H2H pressure and critical data-freshness failures. It does not introduce a second scoring model. When nothing clears those thresholds, it explicitly says there is no urgent action rather than manufacturing one.

The quick cards summarize the Recommended XI, next H2H matchup, strongest supported waiver comparison, availability and transfer position, and four-Gameweek H2H shape. Each card links to the specialist view containing the full evidence. Specialist tabs no longer repeat the global homepage hero: views with their own introduction begin immediately, while simpler views use a compact title.

What the main views are for

  • Overview: urgent, user-relevant decisions and a compact summary of every major decision surface.
  • My Team: your 15 players, the official-lineup view when public Draft data exposes it, a separate Recommended XI and the full squad list.
  • Decision Updates: a persistent inbox of material changes affecting the current actionable Gameweek, plus resolved context and a short decision-cycle archive.
  • Available: free agents sortable by best roster upgrade, upside, floor and other signals.
  • Health & Transfers: actionable squad-health decisions, guarded stash candidates, relevant return dates and expiring transfer evidence with early-pickup guardrails.
  • H2H: opponent scouting, projected XI points, matchup urgency, positional weaknesses, threats, counterweights and guarded tactical moves.
  • League Activity: ownership changes detected between snapshots.
  • Planner: a multi-Gameweek fixture view.

Current limitations

Before a Gameweek locks, public Draft endpoints may not expose the exact editable XI. The toolkit does not invent an official lineup; the Recommended XI is clearly labeled as its own decision-support output. The opponent H2H XI is also an estimate from their owned 15 until exact locked picks are publicly available. Start Score, expected minutes and Projected Points v1.0 remain heuristics rather than calibrated probabilities. Transfer evidence is a small curated overlay rather than an automatic news scraper; it does not infer a role from a fee or a rumour. The model also does not ingest press-conference comments, tactical-role changes, set-piece duties or specialist medical reporting.
